# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: local rural patients and families from Koteshwara, Kundapura, and the surrounding villages of Udupi district in coastal Karnataka. They typically arrive at the site with a job already in hand — a sick relative, a needed diagnostic test, an emergency phone number, a question about whether the hospital handles a particular condition. They are mobile-heavy and frequently on slow 3G connections. Many are more comfortable reading Kannada than English. Their session is short and goal-directed; they are not browsing.

Secondary: distant adult children, NRIs, and extended family researching the hospital on behalf of elderly parents who still live in the region. Their concerns are credibility, doctor specialties, facility quality, and contactability. When a tradeoff arises between the two audiences, local patients take priority.

## Product Purpose

The site is the public face of Dr. N.R. Acharya Memorial Hospital, a 40+ year community hospital founded in 1983 by Dr. N. Bhaskara Acharya in memory of his father, Dr. N. R. Acharya. It serves rural communities in coastal Karnataka with affordable, dignified healthcare across 15+ departments and 24/7 emergency care.

The site exists to:

1. Help local patients reach the right department, doctor, diagnostic service, or emergency phone number with as little friction as possible — measured in seconds on a 3G phone, not in scroll depth.
2. Carry the founder's legacy and the hospital's continuing mission of accessible care, so visitors understand who runs this place and why.
3. Provide the credibility signals that distant family members need to trust the hospital from afar.

Success is patients getting where they need to go quickly, not impressed visitors lingering on a polished landing page.

## Brand Personality

Warm, rooted, neighborly. The hospital is the local family doctor at scale — not a corporate chain, not a Western health startup. Voice is plain, human, and quietly proud of decades of service. It speaks to readers like neighbors, not like prospects.

Three-word personality: **trusted, grounded, gentle**.

Tone notes:
- Plain language, no medical or marketing jargon. "Heart specialist" beats "cardiology consultant."
- Specifics over superlatives. "Since 1983" and "15 departments" beat "world-class care."
- Warmth shows through what is said, not through visual flourish or exclamation marks.
- Founder legacy is mentioned as fact, not as legend. Quiet pride, not hagiography.

Emotional goals: a visitor should feel they are in capable, familiar hands; that the hospital is a real institution rooted in a real place; that they are not being sold to.

## Anti-references

Things this site should NOT look or feel like:

- **Corporate hospital chains** (Apollo, Manipal, Fortis house style). Too polished, too generic, too brand-system-driven; they read as healthcare-as-a-product, not as a community institution.
- **Decorative overload.** Too many pictures, too many boxes, too many side-by-side calls-to-action all fighting for attention. If three things are shouting on a page, the user can't hear any of them.
- **Identical-card grid pages.** Same-sized icon-plus-heading-plus-paragraph cards repeated endlessly as the dominant layout pattern.
- **Western telehealth-startup minimalism.** Stripped-down, illustrated, gradient-laden, "friendly chatbot" energy strips out the institutional warmth this hospital has earned over decades.
- **Template-y or AI-generated feel.** Stock hero imagery, generic medical icons standing in for content, decorative blurs and glassmorphism, gradient text, hero-metric template (big number + label + supporting stats with gradient accent).

## Design Principles

1. **One job per screen.** Every page has a single clear primary action and a defensible reason for its secondary actions. No competing CTAs, no decorative noise. Patients arrive with one task — get them there.
2. **Trust through specifics, not decoration.** Years, names, places, phone numbers, departments, doctor credentials. Concrete details earn trust; stock-photo gloss does not.
3. **Mobile and slow 3G are the design target.** If a section is not usable on a low-end phone with a weak signal, it does not ship. Performance is a brand value here, not a polish task.
4. **Bilingual without compromise.** Kannada is a first-class language alongside English, not a translation overlay. Both versions read naturally and are equally accessible from any screen.
5. **Quiet warmth, not loud charm.** Warmth shows through plain language, human framing, generous spacing, and real portraits — not through gradients, glass effects, animated flourishes, or maximal color.
6. **Refine what exists, do not reboot it.** The current navy + teal + serif system is already on-brand. Future work should sharpen the existing direction toward clarity and professionalism, not replace it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **Kannada / English bilingual** is a hard requirement. The Kannada version is not a translation afterthought; it is a peer experience. Tooling and content workflow must support this from the start.
- **Slow-3G performance budget.** Aggressive image discipline, minimal JavaScript on first paint, system-font fallbacks, lazy loading below the fold. Performance is the most concrete accessibility lever for this audience.
- **No formal WCAG target** has been set. Treat WCAG AA color contrast and tap-target sizing as a working floor, not a ceiling. Re-evaluate once the bilingual content layer is in place.
- **Older patients** are part of the audience even though they are not the primary lens. Body type sizes, line spacing, and tap targets should accommodate readers with average-or-worse near vision on a phone held at arm's length in daylight.
- **Reduced motion** should be respected. Motion is not a load-bearing part of this brand; disabling it should never break comprehension.
